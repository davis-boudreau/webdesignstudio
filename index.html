Example colour palette

An illustrative palette with fixed HEX values. Three primary colours and eight accent colours. These are brand swatches , not interchangeable CSS semantic roles.

Primary brand colours

Accent brand colours

Important distinction:
  • Brand primary describes the example's white, blue and dark grey palette.
  • CSS --primary describes the interface's main action colour; its default is Deep blue.
  • Brand accent describes eight example colours; select one to feature in a UI component.
  • Generated ramps are teaching aids derived from an reference swatch; they are not additional reference colours.
  • Contrast and meaning: an example palette colour is not automatically accessible as text or a button background.

This studio uses an illustrative example palette. Adapt the colours and tokens to your own project requirements.

1. Colour systems

Each ramp has 11 steps. The centre step (500) preserves your chosen HEX value.

RGB mix

Interactive OKLCH colour wheel

Explore hue around the wheel and chroma from centre to edge. Adjust perceptual lightness separately, then apply the colour to a semantic role. The 500 ramp step remains your chosen colour.

Perceptual colour lab
#004780

The wheel shows an sRGB display approximation. At its edges, out-of-gamut chroma is reduced automatically. The ramp generator varies lightness toward its endpoints and gently reduces chroma at the extremes. The example swatches remain unchanged.

Semantic colour roles

How does the ramp generator work?
  • RGB Mix: interpolate each red, green and blue channel toward white for lighter steps, or black for darker steps. Round channels to whole numbers.
  • OKLCH: convert the anchor to perceptual lightness, chroma and hue; vary lightness and gently reduce chroma at the extremes; convert back to sRGB while reducing out-of-gamut chroma.
  • Neither method automatically guarantees contrast or an attractive palette. Review each role and theme.
  • Secondary and accent colours are independently chosen in V2; harmony suggestions are planned for a later iteration.

5. Accessibility quick checks

                

                  WCAG 2.x text contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. This checker measures selected colour pairs only; it does not certify accessibility.
                

                

                

                  Also verify focus visibility, keyboard use, 200% text resize, content reflow, non-text contrast and meaning conveyed without colour.
